Luca, the AI analyst agent

Natural-language questions let you ask about your business in everyday words and get an answer from your data, without writing queries or building reports.

Full Markdown
Home / products / luca agent

Overview

This page describes Luca, the AI analyst agent you use through a chat interface over your connected data. It covers how you ask questions, the kinds of answers you get, and where the agent's limits are. It is for operators and buyers evaluating how Luca would fit day-to-day analysis.

The agent sits on top of Numbers, the reconciled view of your data, and can draw on context you add to the knowledge base. Luca suggests and you decide: it does not change your connected systems.

What this page covers

This page covers asking questions in plain English, answers that combine sources, charts generated from a question, follow-up questions, usage without credits, the web app, recommended next steps, marketing content drafts, the done-with-you working model, and how Luca responds to requests outside what it does.

It does not cover connecting sources, which is described under Data connection, or the reconciled dashboard, which is described under Numbers.

How you work with Luca

Luca is done-with-you, not done-for-you. Luca does the analysis and proposes a strategy. You validate it and carry it out in your own tools.

During onboarding and data reconciliation, Luca's team works alongside you.

AvailableAsk questions in plain English across connected sourcesU84

Natural-language questions let you ask about your business in everyday words and get an answer from your data, without writing queries or building reports.

What is it?

A chat interface where you type a question in plain English and Luca answers using your connected data.

How does it work in Luca?

Luca is used through a chat interface, Luca Web Chat, over your connected data. New users connect a data source and start chatting with Luca.

Questions can draw on whichever sources your account has connected.

How does it help?

It helps you get to an answer without waiting for an analyst or switching between tools. A third-party round-up published in September 2026 described Luca as suited to cross-functional analysis across commerce, marketing, finance, inventory and cash flow.

What happens without it?

Each question means logging in to separate tools, exporting data and combining it yourself.

Who is it for?

Founders, operators and team leads who need answers from their data but do not want to build reports.

How to check it during setup or a trial

Connect two sources, then ask one question that needs both, for example how a recent change in marketing relates to sales. Check whether the answer draws on both sources and whether you can follow it up in the same conversation.

Limits and boundaries

Luca can only answer from sources your account has connected. It answers questions; it does not change anything in those sources.

Evidence

  • Organic users connect a data source and start chatting with Luca. Source: Weekly call, 2026-06-05.
  • A September 2026 third-party round-up (Rivo) describes Luca as suited to cross-functional analysis across commerce, marketing, finance, inventory and cash flow. Source: Rivo / Saras '10 Best AI Analytics Tools for Shopify Brands', 2026-09-26.

Available · limited scopeAnswers that combine marketing, finance and commerce dataU85

Cross-source reasoning means one answer draws on several kinds of business data at once, such as marketing, finance and commerce, instead of reporting from one tool at a time.

Scope: Answers can only span the sources the account has connected and that Luca's team has reconciled.

What is it?

Answers that bring together data from more than one area of your business in a single response.

How does it work in Luca?

Luca is designed as one main orchestrator with tools beneath it. It reasons across your business's sources rather than one tool at a time.

Answers can only span the sources your account has connected and that Luca's team has reconciled.

How does it help?

It addresses a common problem: data is fragmented across tools, with no coherent reasoning layer across them. One answer across areas helps you see how a decision in one area relates to another.

What happens without it?

Each tool reports on its own slice, and you are left to join the pieces and explain any differences yourself.

Who is it for?

Leaders whose decisions span marketing, finance and operations, such as founders, heads of growth and finance leads.

How to check it during setup or a trial

Ask a question that needs marketing and finance data together, then ask a related question about a single source. Compare which sources each answer draws on, and confirm with Luca's team which of your sources are reconciled.

Limits and boundaries

Answers can only span the sources the account has connected and that Luca's team has reconciled. Where a source is connected but not yet reconciled, confirm with Luca's team how it is used.

Evidence

  • Luca's design is one main orchestrator with tools beneath it, reasoning across the business's sources rather than one tool at a time. Source: Weekly call, 2026-05-22 (Eric Bidinger).
  • The problem Luca is positioned against, accepted by Eric: data is fragmented and there is no coherent reasoning layer across tools. Source: Weekly call, 2026-07-17.

To confirm with Luca before publication: Confirm which source combinations are reconciled as standard and attach an example cross-source answer.

AvailableCharts generated from a questionU86

On-demand charts are graphs created in response to a specific question, rather than picked from a fixed set of prebuilt dashboards.

What is it?

Charts and graphs that Luca creates to answer the question you asked.

How does it work in Luca?

When you ask a question, Luca can generate charts and graphs on the fly as part of its answer. Each chart is made for that question, rather than drawn from a fixed set of dashboards.

How does it help?

It helps you see a trend or comparison without first building a dashboard for it.

What happens without it?

You need a prebuilt dashboard for every view you want, or you build the chart yourself from exported data.

Who is it for?

Anyone who prefers to see results visually, including teams presenting figures to colleagues.

How to check it during setup or a trial

Ask for a comparison over time, then ask for the same view broken down a different way. Check that each chart matches the question and that its figures agree with your source.

Limits and boundaries

Charts reflect the data Luca has from your connected sources. This page does not describe exporting or sharing charts; confirm those with Luca's team.

Evidence

  • Luca generates charts and graphs on the fly in answer to a question, rather than from a fixed set of dashboards. Source: Eric call context transcript, ~June 2026.

To confirm with Luca before publication: Attach a screenshot of a generated chart and confirm the chart types and any export options available.

AvailableFollow-up questions in the same conversationU87

Multi-turn follow-ups let you continue a conversation, asking a further question that builds on the previous answer.

What is it?

The ability to keep asking within the same conversation, refining or extending what you asked before.

How does it work in Luca?

Luca is used as an ongoing conversation rather than one question at a time, so you can ask a follow-up that builds on the previous answer. Luca's team has reported users returning to Luca four to five times a day.

How does it help?

It helps you go from a first answer to the detail behind it without restating your question each time.

What happens without it?

Each question starts from nothing, and you repeat context to narrow an answer down.

Who is it for?

Operators who explore a question in steps, such as tracing a change in sales to its cause.

How to check it during setup or a trial

Ask a broad question, then ask two follow-ups that narrow it without restating the first. Check that each answer stays consistent with the one before.

Limits and boundaries

Follow-ups apply within a conversation. This page does not describe Luca remembering earlier conversations; context you want Luca to use belongs in the knowledge base.

Evidence

  • Luca is used as an ongoing conversation; Eric reported users returning four to five times a day. Source: Weekly call, 2026-07-24.

To confirm with Luca before publication: Confirm how much of a conversation Luca carries into a follow-up and attach an example multi-turn exchange.

AvailableUnlimited questions, no AI creditsU92

Unlimited usage means the number of questions you can ask is not capped by a credit or question allowance.

What is it?

Asking Luca as many questions as you need, without counting credits.

How does it work in Luca?

Luca's pricing page lists unlimited usage on every plan. There is no question limit and no credit limit.

How does it help?

You can ask freely, including follow-ups, without rationing questions or watching a balance.

What happens without it?

With credit-based tools, teams may ration questions or skip follow-ups to save credits.

Who is it for?

Teams that ask questions often, and buyers who want predictable usage.

How to check it during setup or a trial

During a trial, use Luca as you would in a normal week, including follow-ups, and check whether any credit counter or question limit appears. Confirm the current terms on the pricing page.

Limits and boundaries

This describes usage as listed on the pricing page. Plans differ in other ways, such as seats; check the pricing page for current plan details.

Evidence

  • The pricing page lists unlimited usage on every plan, with no question or credit limit. Source: ask-luca.com/pricing, read 2026-10-16.

To confirm with Luca before publication: Confirm this wording matches the current pricing page and whether any fair-use terms apply.

AvailableRecommended next stepsU119

Prescriptive next steps are recommendations about what to do, given alongside the analysis that supports them.

What is it?

Recommendations and step-by-step plans that Luca produces alongside its answers.

How does it work in Luca?

Luca produces recommendations and step-by-step plans, not only numbers. It does the analysis and proposes the strategy.

You validate the plan and carry it out yourself.

How does it help?

It helps you move from an answer to a decision, with a plan you can review before acting.

What happens without it?

You get figures but must work out on your own what they mean for your next decision.

Who is it for?

Founders and operators who want a starting point for action that they can check and adapt.

How to check it during setup or a trial

Ask Luca what you should do about a specific issue in your data. Check whether the plan is broken into steps and whether each step traces back to the analysis. Decide which steps you would carry out.

Limits and boundaries

Recommendations are suggestions. Luca does not carry them out; you validate and execute them.

Evidence

Drafts of marketing content that Luca can produce on request.

How does it work in Luca?

Content generation is listed in Luca's product walkthrough and covers posts, newsletters, images and brand assets. It is a secondary capability, and what Luca produces is a draft for your team to review.

AvailableDone-with-you analysis and strategyU127

A done-with-you model means the product does the analysis and proposes a strategy, while your team validates it and carries it out.

What is it?

The way Luca works with you: Luca analyses and proposes, and you decide and act.

How does it work in Luca?

Luca is done-with-you, not done-for-you. Luca does the analysis and strategy, and you validate and execute.

Luca's team also works alongside clients during onboarding and data reconciliation.

How does it help?

It keeps decisions with the people accountable for them, while taking analysis work off their plate.

What happens without it?

Teams either do all the analysis themselves or hand decisions to a tool they cannot check.

Who is it for?

Operators who want analytical help but want to stay in control of what happens in their business.

Luca does not execute changes. The work Luca's team does is part of onboarding and reconciliation; confirm its extent for your account.

AvailableTells you plainly when a request is outside what it doesM1

Clear refusal means an assistant says directly when a request is outside what it can do, rather than attempting it or giving a vague reply.

What is it?

Luca's behaviour when you ask for something it does not do.

How does it work in Luca?

When Luca was asked to automate a change in Shopify or Meta, it replied that it could not. It offered to explain how to make the change, and said a human stays in the loop.

Luca's awareness of its own limits comes from an internal knowledge base written by its team.

How does it help?

It helps you trust what Luca does say, because it is clear about what it will not do.

What happens without it?

An assistant might appear to attempt a task it cannot complete, leaving you unsure what happened.

Who is it for?

Anyone relying on Luca for decisions, and anyone responsible for the systems it connects to.

How to check it during setup or a trial

Ask Luca to make a change in one of your connected systems, such as editing a product. Check that it says it cannot and offers guidance instead.

Limits and boundaries

This describes how Luca responds; it does not mean Luca can make changes on request. The exact wording of a response can vary.

Evidence

  • When asked to automate a change in Shopify or Meta, Luca replied that it could not, offered to explain how to do it, and said a human stays in the loop. Source: Weekly call, 2026-07-24.
