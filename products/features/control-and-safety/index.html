Control and safety: Luca reads, you decide

Control and safety: Luca reads, you decide. Approval by default means no change is made in a connected system unless a person has decided to make it.

Full Markdown
Home / products / features / control and safety

Overview

This page explains what Luca can and cannot do in your connected systems. It is for operators, finance leads and anyone responsible for the systems Luca connects to.

Luca reads, you decide. The agent analyses your data and suggests next steps; you validate them and make any changes yourself.

What this page covers

This page covers two principles: nothing changes in a connected system without you, and Luca is read-only by design.

It does not cover how to connect a source, which is described under Data connection.

What this means in practice

When Luca was asked to automate a change in Shopify or Meta, it replied that it could not. It offered to explain how to make the change, and said a human stays in the loop.

If you ask Luca to change something, expect guidance on how to do it yourself, not the change itself.

AvailableNothing changes without your approvalU137

Approval by default means no change is made in a connected system unless a person has decided to make it.

What is it?

Luca's principle that you stay in control of every change in your connected systems.

How does it work in Luca?

Luca's website states that every action needs approval by default. In practice, Luca suggests and a human stays in the loop.

Nothing changes in a connected system without you, and you make any changes yourself.

How does it help?

It helps you act on suggestions on your own terms, so changes happen only when you decide to make them.

What happens without it?

A tool that changes systems on its own could make changes you did not intend.

Who is it for?

Operators, finance leads and anyone accountable for live systems.

How to check it during setup or a trial

Ask Luca to make a change in a connected system. Check that it does not make the change and instead suggests what you could do.

Limits and boundaries

Luca suggests; it does not make changes in your connected systems on its own. Confirm with Luca's team what the approval-by-default statement covers in the current product.

Evidence

  • Nothing changes in a connected system without the user: Luca suggests, and a human stays in the loop. Source: Weekly call, 2026-07-24.
  • The website states that every action needs approval by default. Source: ask-luca.com, read 2026-10-16.

To confirm with Luca before publication: Confirm what the website's approval-by-default statement refers to in the product today.

AvailableRead-only by designU144

Read-only access lets a system view data in another system without being able to change it.

What is it?

Luca's design principle: it reads your connected data but does not write to it.

How does it work in Luca?

Luca is agent-readable, not agent-writable. It reads your connected data, gets context and reasons over it.

Luca's privacy policy states that its Google access is read-only. Luca's team has said it will not make Luca write to live systems without strong guardrails and audit trails.

How does it help?

It limits what Luca does in your systems to reading, which helps when you assess the risk of connecting them.

What happens without it?

Write access would let a tool change live data, which calls for more trust and review.

Who is it for?

Anyone approving Luca's access to company systems, including IT and finance leads.

How to check it during setup or a trial

When connecting a source, read the permissions the platform asks you to approve. Check whether they are limited to reading, and ask Luca's team about any that are not.

Limits and boundaries

The privacy policy states read-only access for Google specifically. Confirm the access scopes for each other source with Luca's team.

Evidence

  • Luca is agent-readable, not agent-writable: it reads connected data, gets context and reasons. Eric will not make it write to live systems without strong guardrails and audit trails. Source: Weekly call, 2026-05-22 (Eric Bidinger).
  • The privacy policy states Luca's Google access is read-only. Source: ask-luca.com/privacy, read 2026-10-16.

To confirm with Luca before publication: List the access scopes requested for each source and attach the relevant privacy policy section.
